# Enterprise One-, Three-, and Five-Year Strategy Roadmap
## Merchant Sales-Based Financing Strategy Simulator v0.1R2

# 1. Strategic intent

The roadmap is designed for an institution that is new to short-duration processor-linked merchant financing and needs to build a controlled, evidence-driven credit capability before scaling.

The maturity path is:

```text
Year 1: Launch, Control, and Learn
→ Years 2–3: Segment, Optimize, and Automate
→ Years 3–5: Adapt, Expand, and Allocate Dynamically
→ 5+ Years: Enterprise Relationship and Capital Optimization
```

Movement between stages should be governed by evidence-based scale gates rather than calendar passage alone.

---

# 2. Year 1 strategy — Launch, control, and learn

## Strategic objective

Build a safe, measurable initial portfolio; establish product, data, policy, pricing, servicing, and governance foundations; collect reliable performance evidence; and avoid uncontrolled concentration or loss.

## Year 1 operating principle

> Learn safely before scaling.

## Year 1 portfolio posture

- one core processor-linked sales-based financing product;
- expected 30-, 60-, and 90-day payoff bands;
- limited initial industries, partners, and geographies;
- conservative concentration and experiment budgets;
- low-and-grow exposure for uncertain but eligible merchants;
- strong processor-continuity, collateral, covenant, and fraud controls;
- daily performance monitoring;
- explicit exception and manual-review governance;
- pricing tests designed to learn, not to maximize nominal fee immediately.

## Year 1 quarterly roadmap

### Quarter 1 — Foundation and launch readiness

**Product and legal**

- define legal/economic product alternatives;
- approve working terminology and disclosure boundaries;
- define expected payoff, minimum progress, reconciliation, default, and charge-off concepts;
- define collateral, guarantees, liens, and covenant authorities;
- commission product-structure and operating-model legal analysis;
- establish an approved jurisdiction-entry matrix;
- inventory disclosure, licensing/registration, broker, reporting, record-retention, data-segregation, marketing, complaint, collections, and prohibited-contract-feature obligations;
- establish Section 1071 applicability and demographic-data firewall/separation design using then-current official requirements;
- establish bank/nonbank BSA/AML, sanctions, KYB, beneficial-ownership, and suspicious-activity role ownership;
- document payment-data scope and adopt a no-cardholder-data simulator boundary.

**Credit and portfolio**

- establish target market and initial industry appetite;
- establish merchant eligibility and source-data requirements;
- define initial risk tiers and low-and-grow policy;
- define amount, remittance, payoff, and price cards;
- define initial portfolio risk appetite and concentrations;
- define reason codes and decision rights.

**Data and technology**

- establish POS, settlement, deposit, business, owner, fraud, collateral, and covenant data contracts;
- build source registry and data-confidence framework;
- define daily account-performance grain;
- establish deterministic simulation, latest/archive, and scenario controls.

**Governance**

- establish Credit Committee and delegated authorities;
- establish Legal and Compliance product-approval forums and jurisdiction-entry authority;
- establish regulatory-change inventory, source validation, effective dating, and periodic recertification;
- establish policy-change and experiment approval;
- establish model-risk and validation boundaries;
- establish launch-readiness checklist.

### Quarter 2 — Controlled launch and learning portfolio

- launch limited merchant segments and partners;
- use explicit exposure and industry caps;
- implement daily remittance reconciliation and early-warning statuses;
- implement baseline and low-and-grow offer programs;
- launch first controlled tests of amount, remittance percentage, expected horizon, and price;
- monitor fraud, data fallback, manual review, acceptance, first interruptions, early payoff, and loss;
- validate operational handoffs and reason-code evidence;
- validate pre-offer regulatory applicability, disclosure/document package creation, license/registration status, data segregation, and archival evidence;
- prohibit launch in any jurisdiction with stale, unresolved, or unapproved requirement profiles;
- conduct weekly launch review and monthly credit committee review;
- conduct monthly regulatory change and complaint/marketing review during launch.

### Quarter 3 — Early performance learning and targeted refinement

- analyze new versus returning merchant performance;
- distinguish economic deterioration from remittance diversion or data failure;
- refine cash-flow archetypes and industry overlays;
- calibrate early-warning and merchant-health thresholds directionally;
- establish line-reduction, freeze, renewal, and workout pilots;
- refine collateral haircuts and covenant testing;
- evaluate price acceptance and adverse selection by segment;
- remove weak partners or segments where evidence warrants.

### Quarter 4 — Year 1 strategy reset and scale decision

- compare actual performance with launch assumptions;
- review loss, recovery, acceptance, profitability, and concentration by cohort;
- assess experiment results and data quality;
- refresh risk appetite, offer cards, and partner/industry limits;
- define Year 2 growth segments and restricted segments;
- approve scale, pause, or remediation actions;
- publish Year 1 validation and strategy acceptance package;
- recertify each active jurisdiction/product profile and retire unsupported profiles.

## Year 1 critical capabilities

| Dimension | Required Year 1 capability |
|---|---|
| Product | One governed core product with transparent mechanics |
| Underwriting | Explainable policy and risk tiers with low-and-grow option |
| Pricing | Discrete price cards and controlled elasticity tests |
| Collateral | Eligibility, valuation, haircut, lien/control, and LGD treatment |
| Covenants | Five to eight monitored covenant families with actions |
| Performance | Daily expected/actual remittance and payoff-path monitoring |
| Account management | Basic increase, maintain, reduce, freeze, renew, and workout actions |
| Stress | Baseline recession, industry, funding, and partner stresses |
| Risk appetite | Exposure, loss, margin, concentration, operations, and experiment limits |
| Data | Source lineage, as-of controls, reconciliation, and data-confidence tiers |
| Governance | Credit committee, parameter/change control, QA, evidence, and acceptance |

## Year 1 primary KPIs

**Acquisition and decisioning**

- applications;
- data-connection success;
- eligibility rate;
- approval, counteroffer, review, decline, and funding rates;
- straight-through decision rate;
- decision latency;
- exception rate.

**Exposure and structure**

- funded amount;
- facility utilization;
- average remittance percentage;
- expected payoff horizon;
- advance-to-sales;
- residual cash-flow coverage;
- collateral and covenant mix.

**Performance and loss**

- remittance fulfillment;
- repayment progress;
- first interruption;
- minimum-progress shortfall;
- payoff slippage;
- workout entry;
- default, charge-off, recovery, and net loss;
- vintage and cohort performance.

**Economics and market response**

- offer acceptance;
- price gap to competitor;
- gross finance income;
- cost of funds;
- acquisition and servicing cost;
- Expected Loss;
- expected and observed contribution;
- renewal and retention.

**Portfolio controls**

- industry, geography, partner, risk-tier, collateral, and new-merchant concentration;
- low-and-grow exposure;
- high-volatility exposure;
- stacking exposure;
- covenant-breach exposure;
- risk-appetite status.

## Year 1 scale gates

The business should not materially expand until:

1. product and legal structure are approved;
2. source and settlement reconciliation is stable;
3. daily performance statuses are reliable;
4. fraud and processor-diversion controls are functioning;
5. pricing and contribution identities reconcile;
6. collateral and covenant monitoring is operational;
7. loss and early-warning outcomes can be linked to origination evidence;
8. concentration remains within limits;
9. experiment assignments and controls are auditable;
10. policy, validation, and governance evidence supports expansion.

# 3. Years 2–3 strategy — Segment, optimize, and automate

## Strategic objective

Use observed performance to improve risk differentiation, offer design, pricing, operational efficiency, partner management, and relationship treatment.

## Core advances

### Credit and model maturity

- recalibrate risk tiers using observed repayment, default, and recovery;
- develop credit, fraud, acceptance, and recovery challengers;
- introduce merchant-health and time-to-event models where justified;
- differentiate new, repeat, renewed, and previously distressed merchants;
- establish model monitoring, drift, and override governance.

### Merchant-specific precision

- industry-specific cash-flow and seasonality models;
- partner and processor overlays;
- cash-flow archetype policies;
- collateral- and covenant-sensitive offer rules;
- regional and supply-chain sensitivity;
- segment-specific loss mitigation.

### Pricing and offer optimization

- estimate empirical acceptance elasticity;
- measure adverse selection by price and competitor gap;
- optimize amount, remittance, payoff horizon, price, collateral, and covenant bundles;
- introduce relationship and renewal pricing;
- separate acquisition pricing from lifetime relationship value;
- build randomized, multi-generational test governance.

### Account and line management

- automated health segmentation;
- dynamic line increases and reductions;
- temporary seasonal increases;
- prequalified renewals;
- early-warning treatment paths;
- treatment effectiveness and cure tracking;
- partner and relationship-manager actions.

### Operations and technology

- higher straight-through decisioning;
- lower manual-review dependency;
- automated covenant testing;
- integrated workflow and case management;
- real-time or near-real-time event processing;
- governed feature store and monitoring layer.

## Years 2–3 target KPIs

- risk-adjusted contribution;
- marginal approval economics;
- empirical acceptance elasticity;
- renewal and repeat performance;
- treatment effectiveness;
- line-increase and line-reduction outcomes;
- cure and recovery value;
- merchant lifetime and relationship value;
- strategy robustness;
- model and policy drift;
- partner and industry risk-adjusted performance.

## Years 2–3 scale gates

- sufficient mature performance by segment;
- stable empirical model validation;
- documented testing outcomes;
- operational readiness for dynamic account management;
- reliable workout and recovery data;
- demonstrated concentration and stress resilience;
- pricing governance capable of controlling adverse selection;
- executive approval for expanded markets and exposures.

# 4. Years 3–5 strategy — Adapt, expand, and allocate dynamically

## Strategic objective

Transform the portfolio from reactive application decisioning into proactive merchant-relationship and capital management.

## Target capabilities

### Adaptive credit and exposure

- continuous merchant-health monitoring;
- real-time line management;
- merchant-specific exposure and remittance recommendations;
- seasonal and event-based capacity;
- stress-triggered portfolio actions;
- automated renewal and re-underwriting.

### Relationship and wallet expansion

- relationship-level profitability and value;
- integrated merchant services, deposits, treasury, and lending opportunities;
- risk-adjusted retention offers;
- broader facilities for proven merchants;
- prequalified cross-sell and referral actions;
- channel and relationship-manager strategy.

### Portfolio and capital allocation

- stress-adjusted segment contribution;
- marginal capital and concentration allocation;
- industry-dependency and regional network analysis;
- robust strategy selection across multiple futures;
- growth budgets by segment, partner, risk, and relationship stage;
- portfolio optimization under risk-appetite constraints.

### Advanced strategy governance

- enterprise experiment portfolio;
- strategy champion/challenger lifecycle;
- automated evidence and acceptance;
- model and policy retirement standards;
- integrated annual strategic plan and credit budget;
- independent review and audit readiness.

## Years 3–5 target KPIs

- stress-adjusted contribution;
- capital efficiency proxy;
- share of wallet;
- relationship retention;
- prequalified-offer conversion;
- dynamic line-action performance;
- segment resilience;
- concentration headroom;
- economic scenario loss and contribution;
- strategy robustness rank;
- cross-product relationship value.

# 5. Five-plus-year vision — Enterprise relationship and capital optimization

Potential mature capabilities:

- multiple merchant-finance products;
- broader partner and market expansion;
- portfolio acquisition and partnership analysis;
- real-time ecosystem risk monitoring;
- enterprise capital, funding, and liquidity integration;
- advanced AI/ML challengers under formal model governance;
- integrated merchant-services and credit platform;
- strategic portfolio allocation across products, industries, geographies, and channels.

This horizon is a target-state vision, not part of the initial implementation claim.

Target segments and exclusions

Expected loss and contribution targets
Collateral and covenant posture
Experiment portfolio
Line and renewal strategy
Loss-mitigation strategy

# 8. Executive decision cadence

| Cadence | Primary decisions |
|---|---|
| Daily/weekly launch review | Operational breaks, fraud, processor continuity, severe remittance issues |
| Monthly portfolio review | Risk appetite, concentrations, pricing, acceptance, early performance, partner actions |
| Quarterly strategy review | Policy changes, experiments, segment expansion/contraction, line and workout strategy |
| Annual strategy plan | One-year objectives, budget, risk appetite, capability investments, stress plan |
| Three-/five-year refresh | Product, market, technology, model, capital, and relationship strategy |

# 9. Roadmap acceptance standard

A horizon is not achieved because time passed. It is achieved only when its required data, models, controls, operations, validation evidence, governance, and performance thresholds are in place.


# 10. Regulatory capability maturity overlay

| Horizon | Required regulatory capability | Scale implication |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | Approved product-structure analysis, jurisdiction matrix, requirement registry, pre-offer compliance package, manual legal/compliance attestations, complaint and marketing controls | No jurisdiction launches without current approved profile |
| Years 2–3 | Effective-dated rules service, automated applicability, disclosure calculation/versioning, Section 1071 workflow where applicable, record-retention and data-segregation controls, regulatory change regression | Jurisdiction and channel expansion becomes repeatable but remains gated |
| Years 3–5 | Enterprise product-policy orchestration, regulatory impact simulation, cross-entity role mapping, continuous recertification, audit-ready evidence | Supports adaptive multi-product and multi-jurisdiction scale |
| Five-plus | Integrated legal-entity, product, capital, compliance, data, and operating-model planning | Regulatory capability becomes a strategic market-entry constraint and differentiator |
