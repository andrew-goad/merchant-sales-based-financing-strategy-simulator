# M1.8 · Verification, fraud and continuity

**Which evidence concerns identity, fraud or operational continuity rather than credit risk?**

[Stage map](../../README.md#stage-map) · [Which source version?](../SOURCE_VERSIONS.md) · [Current use](../CURRENT_USE.md)

> **Source basis:** original `v0.2R1_ACCEPTED` in `Module_1/1.08_Verification_Fraud_and_Continuity`. This is the retained foundation/Module 1 basis; no Module 1 technical payload is changed by the R26 successor. This is a reading companion, not a new technical acceptance or execution guide.

M1.8 keeps verification, fraud and processor continuity visible as distinct evidence. It does not turn a missing check into a successful check, and it does not call a synthetic fraud score a calibrated credit model.

## Atomic checks and an application summary

The source persists **4,500 atomic verification rows** at application × check × version × as-of timestamp and **750 application summaries** at run × application. Six governed checks cover KYB entity, beneficial owner, sanctions, bank-account match, processor match and fraud screening.

Verification uses its approved source; bank match depends on deposit evidence; processor match depends on POS evidence. The design's default hard-stop treatment for fail/unavailable conditions is evidence for later routing, not an executed adverse action or proof that a real screening provider was queried.

## Separate the questions

**Verification:** what did the relevant check establish, and was its source available?

**Fraud:** which retained signals contributed to the transparent synthetic points—bank mismatch, processor mismatch, identity conflict, abnormal refunds and chargebacks?

**Continuity:** what do accepted processor/connection states show over the eligible operating window after account opening?

A merchant can have different answers to those questions. Collapsing them into a generic risk label would lose the reason why later review or a stop is required.

## Preserve rates; govern interpretation

The accepted methodology is `M1_8_METHOD_V1_1`. Baseline and stress continuity rates are calculated independently from accepted physical histories. The adverse-scenario interpreted tier then applies:

```text
Final stress continuity tier = max(baseline tier,
                                   independently classified stress tier)
```

The original run had 18 interpreted stress-tier improvements. The correction changed those summary interpretations and their hashes, **not the observed rates or 4,500 atomic check rows**. This is a disciplined distinction between observation and policy interpretation, not a license to overwrite uncomfortable source values.

## Downstream meaning and boundary

M1.9 uses this routing and continuity evidence alongside source quality. Later risk components and Module 2 gates can explain a decision using the original reason and source context. M1.8 itself creates neither a final offer nor a production sanctions, AML, identity or fraud certification.

The retained source materializes accepted histories once and uses persisted-row validation rather than invoking prior generators. That architectural choice is documented; no performance benchmark or new runtime qualification is claimed by this reading companion.

## Requirements interpretation

**Design · Checks.** Keep each check tied to its source and default route; missing verification does not become a pass.

**Design · Fraud and continuity methodologies.** Fraud points, credit risk and operating continuity have separate meanings.

**M1_8_POS_48_STRESS_NONIMPROVEMENT.** Apply the accepted interpreted-tier floor while preserving atomic evidence and observed rates.

## What the retained validation establishes

The accepted v0.2R1 package records 60/60 positive controls, 6/6 negatives and zero mismatches across 5,250 canonical entities. `M1_8_POS_48_STRESS_NONIMPROVEMENT` is the disclosed corrected control; its final result has zero improvements, with 462 worsening and 288 unchanged application interpretations. These are synthetic scenario comparisons, not real fraud outcomes.
