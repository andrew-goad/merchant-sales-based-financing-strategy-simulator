# M1.12 · Merchant risk components and integrated proxy

**Can a risk assessment remain transparent about both its drivers and its missing evidence?**

[Stage map](../../README.md#stage-map) · [Which source version?](../SOURCE_VERSIONS.md) · [Current use](../CURRENT_USE.md)

> **Source basis:** original `v0.2R1_ACCEPTED` in `Module_1/1.12_Merchant_Risk_and_Integrated_Proxy`. This is the retained foundation/Module 1 basis; no Module 1 technical payload is changed by the R26 successor. This is a reading companion, not a new technical acceptance or execution guide.

M1.12 combines accepted operating, capacity, confidence, verification, fraud, continuity, industry and relationship evidence into a **synthetic comparative risk proxy**. The result is inspectable because its components remain visible. It is not a calibrated probability of default.

## A score with a physical explanation

The wide grain is **run × scenario × application**. The long grain adds the component. Seven risk-oriented component scores are bounded from 0 to 100, multiplied by governed weights, rounded to six decimals and persisted. The independent integrated score sums the persisted weighted points when all required evidence supports a numeric result.

The normalization is explicit:

```text
Synthetic merchant risk proxy = integrated risk score / 100
```

Dividing by 100 does not turn the number into an empirically calibrated probability. The proxy supplies a bounded comparative input for the downstream synthetic loss/economics chain.

## Floors and unavailable values mean different things

Hard verification stops and fraud tier five impose governed score floors. The adverse scenario applies matched Baseline non-improvement floors to score and tier. Those controls govern interpretation without changing accepted upstream evidence.

A blocked row is different: score and proxy remain **null**, while tier 5 and `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` retain the stopping meaning. It would be incorrect to convert a null proxy into either zero risk or a fully calculated maximum-risk estimate. Complete, partial and blocked states must travel alongside numeric outputs.

## Why components matter to the business explanation

A decision narrative should be able to distinguish weak capacity from an unavailable obligation source and distinguish credit-risk interpretation from a verification stop. M1.12 preserves those source dependencies so later decisioning need not explain every rejection as “the risk score was too high.”

M1.13 uses the usable proxy with its separate exposure/recovery methodology. M1.12 does not define final offer terms, actual default behavior, a reserve or regulatory capital. The component and integrated rows are analytical evidence—not operating authority.

## Inspect the accepted representation

The accepted generation expands 1,500 input paths to **10,500 component rows**, then persists the composite/stress result. Expected rows are compared with actual physical rows, rather than treating a generation-time intermediate as proof of persistence.

The v0.2R1 package designation includes a detail-report revision; it should not be read as a new mathematical model beyond the exact generation and methodology documented in the signoff. Preserve those version roles when tracing the calculation.

## Requirements interpretation

The following are editorial reading locators into the retained requirements/design/authority documents—not invented native requirement IDs. Native IDs and control lists remain in the linked originals.

**Design · Component methodology.** Bound and persist weighted components before summing the integrated score.

**Design · Synthetic proxy.** Preserve the normalized-indicator interpretation; do not label it a calibrated PD.

**Design · Evidence gating.** Keep blocked score/proxy null while retaining explicit insufficient-evidence routing.

## What the retained validation establishes

The retained v0.2R1 signoff records 80/80 positives, 7/7 negatives, 12,000 canonical entities and zero deterministic, composite, proxy-identity or stress-floor violations. Its 1,500 snapshots include 166 complete, 736 partial and 598 blocked. The blocked count is a supported evidence-state outcome, not a quantity of actual customer defaults.
