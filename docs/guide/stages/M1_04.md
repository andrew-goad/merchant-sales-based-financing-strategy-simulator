# M1.4 · Daily POS, settlement and merchant ecosystem

**How does a merchant profile become a traceable operating history?**

> **Source basis:** original `v0.2_ACCEPTED` in `Module_1/1.04_Daily_POS_Settlement_and_Ecosystem`. This is the retained foundation/Module 1 basis; no Module 1 technical payload is changed by the R26 successor. This is a reading companion, not a new technical acceptance or execution guide.

M1.4 moves from a static merchant profile to daily operating evidence. It separately represents sales, transaction quality, processor continuity and settlement timing. Those distinctions later explain why strong sales do not necessarily imply available cash or usable evidence.

## A rectangular panel with explicit inactive days

The physical grain is **population × merchant × processor account × observation date**. The accepted baseline is 750 merchants × 180 dates = **135,000 rows** in `msbf_m1.merchant_pos_daily_base`.

Rows before processor activation remain in the calendar panel as zero-activity, not-connected observations. They are not fabricated transactions. The application set is an integrity prerequisite, but requested funding is not an input that forces sales to support the request.

## Follow the factor chain

Industry daily-sales center is multiplied by merchant scale, weekday/weekend effects, seasonality, trend, lognormal volatility, calendar effects, continuity and bounded operating events. Values are bounded and rounded to currency precision. The deterministic labels separate the components, allowing the same merchant/day to be rebuilt without session randomness.

Seven synthetic generating archetypes—stable, growing, declining, seasonal, volatile, recent disruption and thin history—shape these histories. They are generating assumptions, not the later M1.11 assessed archetypes or calibrated behavioral segments.

## Sales quality and settlement are different transformations

```text
Eligible sales = max(gross sales − refunds − chargebacks − reversals
                    − governed exclusions, 0)
Settlement source date = observation date − settlement delay
Processor fee = settlement amount × processor fee rate
Net merchant proceeds = settlement amount − processor fee
```

The baseline governed exclusion amount is zero. Settlement reads eligible sales from the lagged source date, with a limited pre-window blueprint buffer for the opening observation days. A deposit on a day therefore need not equal that day's gross sales.

Transaction count and average ticket are reconciled after rounding. Processor outage forces zero sales; degradation applies a partial factor. A zero-sales merchant day and disconnected processor evidence remain separately diagnosable rather than being merged into one weak-performance flag.

## What this stage proves—and does not

The expected 135,000-row snapshot is rebuilt from frozen inputs and compared with hashes reconstructed from stored physical columns. Fixed-format scalar serialization prevents numeric display-scale differences from becoming false business mismatches.

M1.5 consumes net proceeds for the deposit/liquidity history. M1.6 overlays scenarios later. M1.4 does not create deposit balances, actual remittance performance, credit decisions, losses or forecasts. Its holiday and operating-event effects are governed synthetic sensitivities, not claims about actual merchant behavior.
