## Three architecture decisions worth following

**Keep evidence and authority separate.** The charter establishes Module 1 as the source of baseline merchant/day histories and origination evidence. Final offer selection belongs downstream. A requested structure can be assessed without being approved.

**Make time part of the contract.** The as-of framework requires evidence to be available at the application/review boundary. Later synthetic lifecycle dates do not become observed historical evidence merely because they are displayed in the same report.

**Keep distinct risk questions visible.** Credit proxy, fraud tier, data confidence and processor continuity answer different questions. A single score must not erase missing evidence or a non-remediable verification stop.

## Requirements as design commitments

The traceability skeleton contains real design IDs such as `DAT-001` (determinism), `DAT-003` (no future data), `DAT-004` (gross-to-eligible sales), `RSK-004` (separate risk dimensions), `GOV-003` (latest/archive) and `GOV-004` (matched comparison). Their detailed implementation and proof must be followed into the actual stage packages.

Other early requirements address later or broader possibilities. Their presence in a charter is **not a claim that allocation, elasticity, covenants, Module 3 operations or every conceptual future module is delivered**. The traceability skeleton also repeats `REG-013` for different subjects; preserve the original and use the full row label when citing it rather than silently inventing a corrected official ID.
