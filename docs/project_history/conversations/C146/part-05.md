**I favor a 12-page carousel, with one change to the old chat’s selection: use page 33 instead of page 30.** That would strengthen the teaching takeaway while preserving the authentic evidence, execution discipline, and separate review that distinguish your work.

R8.1 is now the approved report. The carousel’s job is to give readers a compelling experience of that method—and a reason to explore further.

The story I would tell has two connected movements:

**Prepare the work well enough to delegate substantial execution. Then keep judgment active, including after acceptance.**

That connection is powerful. Page 2 establishes that you can give AI meaningful latitude. The subsequent pages show the preparation, feedback, evidence requirements, and review that make that latitude responsible. Then the monitoring sequence demonstrates that acceptance does not end your responsibility to question the result.

This gives the reader something concrete to recognize and learn from.

**Pages 11 and 26 earn their places.** Removing them to reach ten pages would weaken two important aspects of the professional story: managing execution and subjecting the result to separate challenge. Page 11’s candid language also makes the work recognizable to people who have experienced AI-assisted projects becoming unnecessarily elaborate.

My disagreement with the old chat concerns **page 30 versus page 33**.

Page 30 is valuable in the report. Its chart demonstrates changes in decision categories, but understanding it requires attention to the two taxonomies and their comparison limits. Coming immediately after page 28, it also introduces a different comparison: the full successor decision system, rather than the monitoring calibration.

Page 33 does something particularly useful for this audience: **it turns demonstrated expertise into something the reader can use.** After seeing you frame, challenge, test, and review, readers receive the corresponding questions. That makes the teaching claim tangible. Page 35 then supplies the memorable close.

For the post text, I share your hesitation. “I used AI heavily this year” is a broad opening that could introduce almost anyone’s experience. It spends the first line on usage rather than the judgment demonstrated in this work.

I would lead with **the relationship between delegation and responsibility**. The post should briefly establish:

- You coordinated separate build, reporting, and review conversations.
- You supplied context and refined the strategy before authorizing substantial execution.
- You continued challenging the system after acceptance when its behavior raised a new question.
- The attached pages show the actual interactions and the method readers can reuse.

That is enough framing. The screenshots should carry the professional claims. Readers can see the operating level for themselves without the post announcing that they are looking at an “AI expert.”

I would retain **the cover first and the mandate second**. The cover gives unfamiliar readers their bearings; the next page immediately substantiates it with your own instruction.

And I fully support your format constraint: **unchanged report pages, original 4:3 dimensions, original proportions and typography.** The editorial work belongs in selecting the sequence and writing the accompanying post. Twelve pages is my recommendation because these twelve each perform a distinct job—not because twelve is a universal LinkedIn optimum.
