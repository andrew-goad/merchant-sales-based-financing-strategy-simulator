This is illustrating the remaining-balance cap in the simplified remittance example on Page 2.

Suppose the illustrative terms are:

  • 10% of eligible sales is the remittance share.
  • Today's eligible sales would ordinarily produce a $200 remittance — e.g., $2,000 of sales × 10% = $200.
  • But only $80 remains on the repayment balance.

You would not collect the full $200, because that would exceed the remaining amount due. The simplified formula is:

[ \text{Remittance}=\min(\text{10% of eligible sales},\text{remaining balance}) ]

So:

[ \min($200,$80)=$80 ]

After that $80 remittance, the illustrative remaining balance becomes $0.

Why I put it in the teaching deck

It teaches an important distinction: the sales-linked percentage determines the provisional remittance, but the outstanding balance caps what can actually be collected in this simplified example.

So if the normal calculation says $200 but only $80 remains, you collect $80—not $200 and certainly not a negative remaining balance.

The underlying learning guide describes exactly this simplified rule and explicitly notes that it is illustrative arithmetic, not the R26 remittance generator or a real contract. [export reference: filecite | turn11file0 | L39-L49]

One thing I would improve

Looking at the callout by itself, “$80 remains” is too context-dependent. A learner could reasonably ask, “$80 of what remains?”

I would make it:

THE BALANCE CAP
Only $80 remains to repay

The sales-share calculation produces $200, but the remaining balance caps this illustrative remittance at $80.

That is materially clearer and more pedagogically complete.
