## Product vision

This should be much more than a dashboard. It should become an interactive **portfolio operating and decision-support layer** that moves cleanly from executive conclusions into merchant, application, account, servicing, and source evidence.

The package defines six recommended experiences:

### 1. Executive Portfolio Command Center

A current-state executive view covering:

- merchants, applications, active accounts, funding, exposure, and balance;
- expected versus actual remittance;
- payoff progress and slippage;
- expected loss and risk-adjusted contribution;
- servicing queues and payment exceptions;
- risk, performance, industry, channel, and strategy concentrations;
- one primary and up to two secondary next-month focus recommendations.

### 2. Merchant Lifetime Relationship 360

One selected merchant across:

```text
acquisition source and campaign
→ touchpoints and attribution
→ applications
→ offers and decisions
→ advances and accounts
→ remittances and payoff progress
→ servicing and interventions
→ current exposure
→ accepted synthetic economics
→ relationship or renewal opportunity
```

The initial lifetime view should emphasize **accepted historical synthetic economics**. Any forward-looking lifetime-value estimate must remain clearly labeled as proposed until a governed Module 3 source contract owns it.

### 3. Application & Account Servicing 360

One application/account from request through certified account state:

```text
application request
→ G2 evidence
→ eligibility and policy
→ pricing and counteroffers
→ final decision
→ booking and activation
→ remittance monitoring
→ early warning
→ servicing and intervention
→ payment reconciliation
→ certified account state
```

### 4. Portfolio Focus & Next-Month Action

An as-of-date view that classifies accounts into:

- overperforming;
- stable/current;
- underperforming;
- elevated risk.

The recommendation must be deterministic and explainable. It must state:

- what triggered it;
- how many merchants/accounts are affected;
- affected exposure or balance;
- the intended business objective;
- the recommended management owner;
- supporting measures and reason codes;
- the residual limitation.

It must not imply that the proposed action will causally improve performance.

### 5. Strategy & Scenario Laboratory

Matched strategy and scenario comparisons across approvals, counteroffers, exposure, loss, economics, operational burden, and portfolio trade-offs.

### 6. Governance & Reconciliation

A visible proof page covering:

- source and loaded row counts;
- key uniqueness and orphan controls;
- run, scenario, strategy, and as-of identities;
- G2 and G3 contract status;
- source-to-report reconciliation;
- DAX and visual QA;
- report-release evidence.

A Power BI executive packaging layer was part of the original enterprise design, with explicit pages for portfolio strategy, merchant/offer exploration, daily performance, health, line management, loss mitigation, stress, and long-horizon planning. # Evidence hierarchy for audit conclusions

Classify evidence as:

```text
Class A
Accepted source, contract, formal sign-off, executed primary evidence,
physical hash identity, or accepted catalog.

Class B
Machine-readable validation result, independently reproducible count,
reconciliation output, checkpoint, or deterministic audit reconstruction.

Class C
Design artifact, BRD, architecture, semantic model, mapping, test plan,
or implementation document not yet runtime-proven.

Class D
Chat narrative, summary, assertion, screenshot, or interpretation without
the underlying artifact.
```

Rules:

- A technical PASS should normally require Class A or B evidence.
- A design-freeze PASS may rely on Class C only when the required design
  artifacts are complete, internally consistent, and not contradicted by
  higher authority.
- Class D evidence alone cannot close a technical or governance finding.
- Screenshots may support review but do not replace source files, logs, or
  machine-readable evidence when those should exist.

# Severity classification

## Critical

Examples:

- accepted source or contract was modified without authority;
- live execution occurred without authorization;
- accepted certification database was used as a campaign target;
- recovery was inserted into the normal execution chain;
- production, causal, regulatory, or autonomous claims were made;
- a false contract or gate PASS was asserted;
- a canonical identity was broken across workstreams.

## High

Examples:

- fact grain or key mismatch;
- duplicate truth ownership between Module 2 and Module 3;
- Power BI creates an unsupported durable business definition;
- campaign expected results do not match the frozen configuration;
- M3 SQL begins before charter reconciliation;
- a workstream omits a required validation or acceptance artifact;
- a shared definition changes without downstream propagation.

## Medium

Examples:

- incomplete lineage;
- weak source-to-visual mapping;
- missing reason-code coverage;
- incomplete negative testing;
- documentation or catalog mismatch;
- open limitation not disclosed prominently.

## Low

Examples:

- wording, formatting, naming, navigation, or presentation defect that does
  not change interpretation or execution.
