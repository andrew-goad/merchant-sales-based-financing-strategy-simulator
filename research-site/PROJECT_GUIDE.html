From a financing question to an account balance

A business needs working capital now and can commit part of its future daily sales to repayment. How much can it receive? How much cash would the agreement collect if sales slow? The simulator follows both decisions for the same synthetic merchant. The operator chooses the population and policy, the engine evaluates the request, and the reports trace each result back to a decision or an account-day.

Trace one requirement to one payment

In the 750 · Local GUI study, merchant 000462 owed $1,486.49 when day 111 began in Baseline. Eligible sales of $31,024.48 would have produced $3,030.22 at the contracted 9.7672% remittance rate. The balance cap reduced the actual collection to $1,486.49 and closed the account. Under Recession Energy, that day’s eligible sales were $21,447.95, the collection was $2,094.86, and $14,894.22 remained. The same rule produced different payments because the sales and opening balances differed. Inspect days 110–111 in the ledger · Read the balance cap in SQL

Test an offer policy

A business requesting $20,000 could receive an $8,000 counteroffer under a 40% minimum. Raising that minimum to 60% makes the smallest permitted offer $12,000. In the 750 Local GUI study, 45 of 190 selected Baseline counteroffers and 10 of 48 under stress fell below 60%. These counts include offers sent for review. In the separate 25,000 study, 190 of 522 authorized Baseline counteroffers fell below the proposed minimum. These historical counts identify cases for investigation. To test the policy, generate a new reference, save its population and reuse it for the challenger. Raise the minimum offer from 40% to 60% and lower the linked maximum reduction from 60% to 40%. Hold all other settings fixed. Compare replacement offers, referrals, declines and outstanding balances in the two runs. Selected counteroffers by study
