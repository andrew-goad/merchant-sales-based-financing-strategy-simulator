What can you change in a financing experiment?

Using the campaign console and its parameter guide

Spoken transcript

Would the same businesses qualify if a lender considered only shorter payoff horizons? In the campaign console, you can change the policy and keep the merchants the same.

For a new population, choose 750 to 25,000 merchants. 57 settings shape the industry mix, daily sales, cash buffers, financing requests and how sales respond to recession stress.

10 more controls govern lending policy. Set the smallest counteroffer, choose eligible payoff horizons, or specify which risks need an underwriter. You can also limit how much total repayment may increase.

The parameter guide covers 180 entries, including fixed settings. Look up processor tenure minimum. It gives the allowed range in months and explains how tenure depends on business age.

I cap business age at 24 months, then require 36 months with a payment processor. Both numbers are allowed individually. Together, they require a business to have processed sales before it existed. The console flags the conflict.

I lower the processor minimum to 12 months. The ranges overlap and the conflict clears. Policy settings have checks too. A 3% automatic cost increase exceeds a 2% review limit. The run still checks the generated data.

To test shorter offers, reuse a saved population and choose Challenger. Keep 30 and 60 days eligible. Turn off 90. Now you can compare who qualifies and who needs review using the same merchants and sales histories.

On-screen sources and figures

0:00 – The campaign console
The study asks whether restricting eligible payoff horizons would change the decisions for the same businesses. The console includes both Baseline and Recession Energy in each campaign.

0:12 – A new population
“Define your cohort” offers 750, 2,500 and 25,000 presets. The number of merchants accepts any whole number from 750 through 25,000. The new-population editor has five groups of adjustable definitions. The examples pair Population design with Industry mix, POS generation with Daily sales center, Deposit and liquidity with Balance buffer center, Application and product with Minimum request, and Scenario overlays with Sales-level shock. The 57 definitions can apply at different scopes, such as industry and size.

0:29 – Policy choices
“Offer access” shows a minimum of 40% of the request paired with a maximum reduction of 60%. “Risk eligibility” shows an automatic tier ceiling of 2 and a review ceiling of 3. “Repayment cost guardrails” sets the permitted increase over the repayment originally requested. “Structure search & human-review triggers” includes the 30-, 60- and 90-day candidate horizons. There are 10 supported strategy controls.

0:44 – Find a setting in the guide
The searchable parameter guide includes 180 entries. The entry “Processor tenure minimum” describes how long merchants have had their processor relationship. It accepts 1 to 240 whole months, with a registered default of 3 months. The guide explains that the tenure must fit the possible business ages.

0:59 – Configuration review identifies a conflict
“Business age maximum” is set to 24 months. “Processor tenure minimum” is set to 36 months. “Configuration review” shows “No campaign has started. Submission blocked.” It identifies one blocking conflict. With ages ranging from 6 to 24 months, every possible merchant is too young. The error suggests lowering the processor minimum or raising the business-age maximum. The “Review & run” button is disabled.

1:17 – A correction and a second example
The processor minimum changes from 36 to 12 months. With the business-age maximum still 24 months, the ranges overlap. The form reports “Configuration ready for review.” A separate policy example sets the automatic repayment-cost allowance to 3% and the review allowance to 2%. The automatic allowance must be at most the review allowance. Generated merchant records undergo additional checks during execution.

1:35 – Prepare a policy comparison
The closing example loads the saved baseline record from the 25,000-merchant campaign and selects Challenger. The generation settings are locked. The eligible payoff horizon choices keep 30 and 60 days and remove 90 days. The comparison reads “Candidate payoff horizons” and shows 30, 60, 90 changing to 30, 60. Changing which offers the strategy may consider preserves the merchants’ original requested horizons. The screen illustrates the proposed policy comparison. A completed challenger run would be needed to observe financing or review outcomes.
