Where can a bank keep lending in a recession, and what risk remains? I kept the credit policy fixed across both scenarios.

In the console, I selected EC2 Windows, set the cohort to 750 synthetic applicants, and chose Amazon S3 for the results.

I submitted the campaign once. The console refreshed progress every 30 seconds as each application went through Baseline and Recession Energy.

On AWS, about 45 minutes of computation produced 1,500 decisions and 16,320 daily monitoring records.

In Baseline, 318 businesses were funded or referred for review. Under stress, that fell to 72. Tableau shows how many actually received financing. 110 in Baseline, 26 under stress.

In Baseline, the lender advanced $1.57 million. Under stress, that fell to about $491,000.

Twenty-three businesses got their full request either way. Merchant 000225 received $150,000 for inventory, with 8.68% of eligible sales going toward repayment.

By day 120, this business still owed about $20,000 in Baseline. Under stress, it owed about $1,350 more on the same advance.

The stress account went into servicing review. Its booked terms stayed in force.

Twenty-five accounts paid off. One merchant carried all the remaining exposure. That concentration matters when deciding where to extend credit as conditions change.
