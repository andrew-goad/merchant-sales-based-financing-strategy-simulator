When does a decision become a funded account?

Inside the code and controls of Merchant Sales-Based Financing

Spoken transcript

The engine proposes two counteroffers. One offer is authorized. The other needs an underwriter’s review. How would you check that only the authorized offer became a funded account?

On Windows, PowerShell starts the local console. The HTML form collects the experiment settings. JavaScript sends them to Python, which checks the request and starts a worker. That worker coordinates the stages. PostgreSQL runs the SQL rules and records the results. Power BI and Tableau let us inspect them.

The folders follow the work. Module 0 establishes the run and its inputs. Module 1 builds the evidence for each application. Module 2 takes that evidence through underwriting, booking and repayment monitoring.

Open the booking stage, Module 2.4. Its business requirement says funding starts with an authorized offer. The SQL reads the previous stage’s decision and sends a counteroffer awaiting review down a separate path.

The stage test tries to put $1,000 on an inactive record in a temporary table. The database rejects the row. That rejection is what earns a pass. You can inspect both the test and the saved result.

I checked the decisions against the funding records in the local campaign. 750 merchants, two scenarios each. All 150 authorized decisions led to funding. 265 decisions went to review. None produced an advance.

That tells me the engine honored the funding rule in this run. Was the rule a good one? Next I’d compare referrals with underwriters’ reasons, and funded offers with actual sales, operating expenses and repayments. Could merchants repay and still make payroll?
