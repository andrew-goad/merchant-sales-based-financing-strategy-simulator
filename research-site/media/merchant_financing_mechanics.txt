Merchant Sales-Based Financing
How a business repays an advance from daily sales

Spoken transcript

Merchant Sales-Based Financing. A business needs money to repair its equipment. A lender advances the money, and the business repays it with a percentage of its daily sales.

In this simulation, the business receives $146,600. With the fixed financing charge, it agrees to repay just over $184,000.

The agreed share is just under 10% of eligible sales. On day 1, that comes to about $2,400.

On day 3, the business has no eligible sales. Its payment is zero, so its balance stays the same.

On day 111, the share of that day’s sales comes to about $3,030. How much does the business still owe?

Only $1,486.49. That’s the final payment, and the balance reaches zero.

This simulated business received $146,600.00 for equipment repair. The fixed financing charge was $37,863.41, bringing agreed repayment to $184,463.41. The contract collected 9.7672% of eligible daily sales, capped at the opening balance.

Baseline day 1. Eligible sales of $24,302.06 produced a $2,373.63 payment.
Baseline day 3. Eligible sales and the payment were both $0.00. The balance remained $180,074.46.
Baseline day 111. Eligible sales were $31,024.48. The sales share rounded to $3,030.22. Only $1,486.49 was still owed, so the final payment was $1,486.49 and the account closed at $0.00.
