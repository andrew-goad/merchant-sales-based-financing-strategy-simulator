What makes a sales-based financing offer workable?

The research behind Merchant Sales-Based Financing

Spoken transcript

An Illinois business owner told Federal Reserve researchers, “I hope to see rates, terms and what I qualified for.” When repayment comes from daily sales, what will be left for payroll on a slow week?

Barbara Lipman and Ann Marie Wiersch asked 42 owners to compare sample offers. Many struggled to understand the costs. When the researchers reviewed 10 online lenders’ websites, some cash advance providers gave no price at all.

EXL asked how lenders could design better offers for shoppers at checkout. Its researchers recommended testing amount, rate and term across different customers. I borrowed that method to explore offers for the merchant.

Russel, Shi and Clarke studied why payment processors can finance merchants. Eight months after funding, recipients processed 16% less through one South African provider than similar firms that passed. Who sought financing and where merchants routed sales both mattered.

Those studies inspired me to follow an offer through to repayment. In one simulation, the same merchant took identical terms. It paid in full under Baseline. Under energy stress, it still owed about $3,700 on day 120.

The Fed has no dedicated merchant cash advance rule. For state member banks making loans, its standards call for documenting the repayment source and assessing whether the borrower can pay.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau excludes qualifying merchant cash advances from Section 1071 reporting. Other fair-lending duties depend on whether the agreement is credit.

The Reserve Banks expect new small-business survey results in 2027. My next test is whether the offers that work in simulation also leave real merchants enough cash to operate and repay.
