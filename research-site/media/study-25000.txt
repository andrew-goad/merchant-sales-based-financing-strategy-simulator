When can a higher payoff rate hide repayment risk?
25,000 · AWS GUI

When can a higher payoff rate hide repayment risk? I tested the same 25,000 simulated businesses, with repayment tied to daily sales.

On AWS, I used PowerShell to open an early version of the campaign console.

In the browser, I submitted the campaign for 25,000 merchants and watched its progress.

50,000 decisions and over half a million daily monitoring records took 15 hours and 26 minutes of computation.

Tableau shows thirty-three hundred and thirty-seven businesses funded in Baseline. Under Recession Energy, 1,005 receive financing.

At day 120, about 5.3% still owed money in Baseline. Under stress, only 4.3% did.

Now follow the 1,005 businesses funded both times. 36 still owed money in Baseline, 43 under stress.

Why the reversal? 140 open Baseline accounts belonged to businesses that received no financing under stress.

In Power BI, we see this restaurant received $150,000 both times. The same share of eligible daily sales went toward repayment.

Payments matched through day 60. The shock reached collections on day 61. Baseline paid off on day 118.

Under stress, it still owed about $10,700 on day 120.

Before expanding credit on the strength of a payoff rate, I’d check who got funded and how those same businesses repaid.
