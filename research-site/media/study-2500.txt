When sales weaken, who still gets funded and for how much?
2,500 · AWS PowerShell

When sales weaken, who still gets funded and for how much? I tested 2,500 simulated businesses with advances repaid from daily sales.

I launched Module 1 from PowerShell on AWS. It verified the inputs and returned a checkpoint.

Module 2 picked up that checkpoint the next day and followed each funded account for 120 days.

Together, 5,000 decisions took 2 hours and 24 minutes of computation.

In Tableau, 329 businesses receive funding in Baseline. 100 of them also receive funding under Recession Energy.

Of the other 229, 21 went to review and 208 were declined.

Those businesses explain over 99% of a $3.35 million gap. Four smaller offers explain the rest.

Take merchant 002191. Power BI shows this online business received the full $22,800 it requested for expansion.

Under stress, it received $9,200 at the same share of daily sales.

The stress model first selected $18,200. The final policy ruled it out because its share of sales fell below Baseline.

The smaller advance paid off on day 37, before the shock reached collections. The larger Baseline advance took 91 days.

Could collecting a smaller share of daily sales keep more financing within reach? I’d test whether those larger advances still repay.
