How did a smaller advance fare under stress?
750 · Local DBeaver

Same business. Same inventory request. Under stress, the lender offers less than half. Which advance pays off first?

I ran this study in DBeaver, one SQL program at a time. First, I checked the prior stage had passed and the right policy was loaded.

If we fund this advance, what could it contribute? The calculation accounts for costs, expected losses, and the cost of putting capital at risk.

A separate program checked the saved results. If the amounts disagreed, I could trace the failed check back to the figures.

I tested what should go wrong, too. Disabling the policy had to stop the calculation. The next stage received the results and the evidence behind them.

Tableau pairs the same 750 applications. 103 received financing in Baseline and 27 under stress. Two full approvals became counteroffers.

Merchant 98 was one. In Power BI, Baseline funded the full $5,200. 10.9% of eligible sales went toward repayment.

Under stress, the lender offered $2,500. The same share of daily sales now went toward a smaller balance.

Follow the two balances. The smaller advance paid off on day 85. On day 120, the larger advance still had over $1,700 outstanding.

Did this merchant do better under stress? Both the sales path and the offer changed. That matters when comparing repayment.
