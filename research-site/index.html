Merchant sales-based financing

When repayment follows sales

Andrew Goad · Strategy, simulator design and analysis

Abstract In a study of 750 simulated businesses, one merchant received the same $146,600 equipment-repair advance in both scenarios. It paid in full on day 111 in Baseline and still owed $3,743.44 on day 120 under energy stress. Funding fell from 115 businesses to 35. The share still owing fell across the two funded portfolios and rose among the same 35 merchants.

Abstract In this 750-business AWS study, 110 merchants received financing in Baseline and 26 under energy stress. Follow the inventory advance that accounted for the stress portfolio’s remaining balance, then examine what a declining sales trend means for the same businesses and dates.

Abstract This AWS PowerShell campaign evaluated 2,500 businesses. Funding fell from 329 in Baseline to 100 under energy stress. A healthcare business received the same $150,000 advance in both scenarios. Tracking its collections reveals how weaker sales can leave more to repay.

Abstract This AWS GUI campaign evaluated 25,000 businesses. Funding fell from 3,337 in Baseline to 1,005 under energy stress. The share still owing on day 120 fell across the two funded portfolios and rose among businesses funded in both. A restaurant’s daily ledger shows how repayment unfolded under the same terms.

In Federal Reserve studies, business owners struggled to compare financing offers. I built MSBF to trace how a lender advances cash, how a share of daily sales repays it, and what happens when sales weaken. The five campaigns use simulated businesses. Research and references

What changes when the minimum counteroffer rises from 40% to 60%?

Two merchants in this historical DBeaver study received smaller advances under stress. A higher floor could change which alternative offers the engine can consider.

Which sales days did the engine replay?

Collection days 1–60 replay days 61–120 of the sales history. Collection days 61–120 replay history days 121–180. The lender has already seen the full history when it sets the offer. Different advance amounts or remittance rates can therefore produce different balances from the start. On each collection day the model applies the agreed percentage of eligible sales, up to the amount still owed. A zero-sales day produces a zero payment. Each chart shows the end-of-day balance. A balance still due on the last day leaves the eventual payoff date open.

How the two sales scenarios were modeled

In the local 750-merchant GUI run, the stress affected the final 60 days of a 180-day history. The sales adjustment depends on the merchant’s industry and its exposure to energy costs. The model also varies sales by merchant and day. Those fluctuations repeat when the same population and settings are replayed. A business can therefore have a stronger individual sales day during an overall decline. Deposits, refunds and chargebacks also affect the evidence behind an offer. The lender applies the same policy in both economic settings. Some businesses receive different terms, some go to review and some lose access to financing. The simulation then follows collections on each funded advance.

Why one of the 34 paid off a day earlier under stress

Merchant 000499 received the same $11,100 advance in both scenarios. Twenty-eight matched merchants paid off on the same day and five paid off later under stress. This was the only merchant to pay off earlier.

The scenario specifies an 18% sales-level shock. Professional Services gets 10% of that shock, giving this merchant a 1.8% reduction in gross sales before daily variation. A second part of the model moves sales up or down for each merchant and date. The population and seed fix those fluctuations, so replaying the experiment gives the same two days.

Run the experiment on Windows

Follow the steps in order on a Windows desktop or Windows EC2 instance. In the GUI, Logic & impact and the Parameter guide explain each setting. Configuration review checks combinations as you edit and points to the fields that need attention before submission.

Download the A3 Windows operator ZIP (342.67 MiB) · Operator SHA-256. Save the operator in Downloads. Step 2 verifies the ZIP and extraction. The source archives on GitHub contain the repository.

1. Install Python, PostgreSQL, Tableau and Power BI
  • CPython for Windows. Choose the 64-bit installer. The examples use Python 3.14 at C:\Python314\python.exe. Set the path in both PowerShell windows if yours differs.
  • PostgreSQL 17.11 for Windows x64. Install PostgreSQL Server and Command Line Tools. Keep the tools’ bin directory. The example creates a dedicated UTF-8 cluster on port 5437 alongside any existing service.
  • Tableau Desktop or Tableau Desktop Public Edition. Install an edition that opens packaged workbooks locally.
  • Power BI Desktop (64-bit). Install Desktop with Power BI Project (PBIR) support.

The Power BI patch uses Analysis Services client libraries. If your Desktop installation lacks them, run this command in 64-bit Windows PowerShell. Accept the NuGet provider prompt if it appears. SqlServer module.

Install-Module -Name SqlServer -RequiredVersion 22.4.5.1 -Scope CurrentUser -Repository PSGallery

    This first-time block creates dbdata and starts PostgreSQL on loopback. Keep the database password you choose for setup and campaign admission.     if (Test-Path -LiteralPath $pgData) { throw 'The database directory must be new.' }

A balance still due on the last day leaves the eventual payoff date open.

The research behind this study connects the cost information available before financing with sales and collections after funding.

  1. Barbara J. Lipman and Ann Marie Wiersch (2018, 2019). Federal Reserve studies of how owners compare financing offers.

    A co-owner of an Illinois architecture firm wanted to compare prices, see the repayment terms and know what the firm could qualify for before sharing contact details. The co-owner was one of 42 small business owners in the focus groups. The researchers later examined 10 online lenders’ websites. Some gave no price for their merchant cash advances. In MSBF, readers can follow a simulated applicant from the amount requested to the terms offered, then see how much those terms collect from daily sales. Read the 2018 focus-group study (PDF, pp. 1–2, 15) and the 2019 website study (PDF, pp. 6, 15–16).

  2. Dominic Russel, Claire Shi and Rowan Clarke (2025). Fintech and financial frictions: the rise of revenue-based financing. South African Reserve Bank working paper.

    Eight months after taking financing, merchants processed 16% less through the studied payment provider than similar merchants who did not take financing. The authors could observe the provider’s transaction volume. Total business revenue may follow a different path. MSBF likewise distinguishes eligible sales when calculating daily remittance. Read the SARB working paper.

  3. Vikas Sharma, Saurabh Gangwal and Ankit Maheshwari (2023). Creating a successful risk management framework for point-of-sale financing. EXL industry white paper.

    EXL describes randomized tests of offer amount, rate and term for shoppers borrowing at checkout. In MSBF, the merchant receives the advance. The comparable research question is how changing an offer affects access to financing and repayment from sales. Read EXL on testing offers (PDF, p. 9).

U.S. rules and work in progress

As of November 25, 2026

How do businesses see the price?

The Federal Reserve’s March 2025 research explains factor-rate pricing and collection from a share of sales. The publication sets no merchant-cash-advance pricing or disclosure rule. Regulation Z generally exempts business-purpose credit. State disclosure law may still apply. In New York, a covered provider making a specific sales-based financing offer must disclose the financing charge and estimated annual percentage rate, among other terms. Read the Fed’s March 2025 research, Regulation Z § 1026.3(a)(1) and New York Financial Services Law § 803.

The calculator caps the final payment at the outstanding balance. A real case shows why that control matters. Yellowstone settled FTC allegations that included withdrawals after merchants had finished repaying. Read the FTC’s 2021 settlement announcement.

How would a bank document repayment capacity?

For a state member bank making a loan, Federal Reserve Regulation H incorporates safety and soundness standards. Appendix D-1 calls for documenting the loan’s purpose and repayment source and assessing the borrower’s capacity to repay on time. For a sales-linked payment, a practical question is how much cash is available to remit on a weak sales day. Regulation H § 208.3(d)(1) and Appendix D-1, II.C.

Which transactions does the federal data rule cover?

Effective June 30, 2026, the CFPB excluded defined merchant cash advances from Section 1071 small-business application data collection and reporting. The definition covers a lump sum exchanged for a share of future sales or income up to a cap. Whether an arrangement is credit under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act depends on its terms and operation. Covered credit is subject to Regulation B’s anti-discrimination rule and applicable business adverse-action notice requirements. The CFPB plans to monitor this market and could reconsider the exclusion in light of new evidence. Read the 2026 final rule and Regulation B § 1002.4 and § 1002.9.

What work is underway?

The Reserve Banks expect reports from their voluntary 2026 Small Business Credit Survey in 2027. Separately, the Federal Reserve and peer agencies requested comment on proposed nonbinding third-party risk guidance for banks and credit unions, with comments due December 16, 2026. The proposal addresses third-party relationships broadly, including the kind of reliance on a payments processor a bank might examine here. See the survey schedule and read the agencies’ proposal announcement.
